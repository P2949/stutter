# Idle CPU of the deployed services, repeated measurement (2026-11-09)

Source `4f3baa07`, binary sha256 `bf79d9c4…20d8a6`. Responds to the review note that the
production-smoke figure of 4.97% against the 5% ceiling was a single, narrow-margin reading.

**Method.** Five consecutive 60 s intervals; CPU is the sum of `utime + stime` clock ticks of the two
service processes (`stutter-autotune-bounded@p2949`, `stutter-privileged-worker@p2949`, main pids
read from systemd) read from `/proc/<pid>/stat` at the interval edges, divided by wall time. Nothing
else was started during the run (no builds, no tests); the desktop session was logged in.

| interval | CPU (% of one core) | 1-min load average |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.966 | 2.74 |
| 2 | 1.900 | 2.47 |
| 3 | 1.983 | 1.80 |
| 4 | 2.100 | 2.99 |
| 5 | 2.000 | 2.49 |

Mean 1.99%, range 1.90–2.10%.

**What this does and does not show.**
- It shows the deployed daemon and worker use about 2% of a core at rest, stable to about ±0.1
  points over five intervals, with a 3-point margin to the ceiling.
- It is *not* the production-smoke measurement: that check runs its own predeclared window under
  its own load and counts what it counts, and it reported 4.97% (the previous binary measured 4.85%
  under the same smoke load). The two figures are not comparable and neither replaces the other.
- The host was not quiet (load average 1.8–3.0 from the desktop session), so this is "at rest under
  an ordinary desktop", not an isolated idle baseline.
- No regression was demonstrated, so no sensor cadence or threshold was changed; the 5% ceiling is
  unchanged. Per-sensor attribution was not done; if the smoke figure rises again, that is the next
  step.
